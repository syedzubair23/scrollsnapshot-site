Make the important part obvious

Annotation turns a capture into an explanation. Draw a shape around a faulty control, point an arrow at the exact state, number the steps, or put a short label where a reader will look first. This guide is a map of the whole editor; the guides it links to go deeper on each part.

01

Open the editor

Capture something, then choose Edit on the result page. The editor opens over the same capture. Nothing you draw changes the file until you press Apply, so you can experiment freely and leave with Cancel.

02

The tool row

Along the bottom, from left to right: Select, Redact, Shape, Line, Highlight, Text, Pen, Emoji, and Step numbers (turn it on in Settings, under Features, if it is hidden). After the tools come the colour and stroke buttons, then zoom, Undo and Redo, and finally Cancel and Apply.

Shape, Line, and Redact each have a small menu beside them for their styles. Text, Highlight, and Pen open a panel of options from the button beside the colour.

03

Which tool for what

  1. Shape: outline a control, a button, or a region. Five shapes, from one menu.
  2. Line: point at something with an arrow, show a link between two things with a double arrow, or underline with a plain line.
  3. Highlight: emphasise a line of text, as a block, an underline, or a strikethrough.
  4. Text: put a short label where a reader will see it first. Four fonts, three sizes, and a background chip or outline for legibility.
  5. Pen: sketch freehand, with solid, dashed, or dotted strokes and smoothing.
  6. Step numbers: number a sequence of actions in one image. The numbers follow the markers, so deleting one renumbers the rest.
  7. Emoji: place a check mark, a warning or any other emoji with a click.
  8. Redact: cover private details with Black out, Pixelate, or Blur. This one hides content; the others only point at it.
04

Marks stay aligned with the image

ScrollSnapshot stores mark positions in the image's own pixels. A tall screenshot may be displayed small on screen, but a box or arrow is mapped back to the full image before export, so zooming the view never moves a mark relative to what it points at.

05

Marks stay editable

A mark is not fixed the moment you draw it. Pick it up with the Select tool (V) to move, resize, rotate, restyle, duplicate, or delete it before you apply anything. The guide to editing marks covers the handles, the keyboard shortcuts, and the colour picker with its eyedropper.

06

Colour and stroke

The colour button opens twelve named colours and a full picker with an eyedropper (I) that takes a colour straight from the capture. The stroke button sets how thick lines and outlines are. With nothing selected they set the next mark; with a mark selected they change that mark. Style presets save a favourite combination so you can recall it with one click.

07

Apply, Cancel, and undo

Undo and Redo step through every change you make in the editor, including mark edits. Apply writes your marks into the image and closes the editor. Cancel leaves without changing anything. After Apply, the result page's own Undo edits restores the capture as it was.

08

A readable sequence

  1. Open Edit and decide what the reader should see first.
  2. Use one colour and a small number of marks to establish the reading order.
  3. Zoom in to place an edge precisely, and use Shift to keep shapes square and lines level.
  4. Apply the marks, then review the final file at the size your recipient will see.
KEEP IN MIND

Redactions are painted before other marks, so a later arrow cannot uncover information you covered.
