Draw the outline you need

A rectangle is not always the right outline. The Shape tool draws a rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, or diamond, and the Line tool draws an arrow, a double arrow, or a plain line. Both live in the editor's tool row, each with a small menu beside it.

01

Find the tools

Open a finished capture and choose Edit. The tool row along the bottom runs from the Select tool, through Redact, Shape, and Line, to Highlight, Text, Pen, and Step numbers. Shape and Line each have a small chevron beside them: that is where their styles are.

The colour and stroke buttons come next, then zoom, Undo and Redo, and finally Cancel and Apply. Nothing is written into the image until you press Apply, so you can keep adjusting.

02

Shapes, from one menu

Press B, or choose the Shape tool, then open the menu beside it and pick a rectangle, a rounded rectangle, an ellipse, a triangle, or a diamond. Drag over the capture to draw it. The shape comes up selected, ready to be resized.

The same menu changes a shape you have already selected. If you drew a rectangle and decide an ellipse reads better around a button, select the rectangle and choose Ellipse. You do not need to redraw it.

  • A rounded rectangle looks softer around a button or a card than a sharp-cornered box.
  • A diamond or a triangle is a good way to flag a warning without covering what is inside it.
03

Lines and line ends

Press A or L for the Line tool. Its menu offers an arrow, a double arrow with a head at each end, and a plain line. Use an arrow to point at one thing, a double arrow to show a distance or a relationship between two things, and a plain line to underline or divide.

As with shapes, choosing a different style while a line is selected changes that line.

04

Keep it straight

Hold Shift while you drag. A shape stays square, and a line snaps to 45-degree steps, which is the quick way to get a level underline or a perfectly vertical pointer.

  1. Choose the Shape or Line tool and pick a style from its menu.
  2. Drag over the capture, holding Shift where the mark should stay square or level.
  3. Press V and pick the mark up if you need to move or resize it, then press Apply.
KEEP IN MIND

A shape or line only points at content. It does not hide it. Use Redact when the pixels themselves must be covered.
